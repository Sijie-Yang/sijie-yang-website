  在 Substack 上，我写过一篇关于街树与城市微气候的短随笔。核心观点很简单：**街树不只是景观元素，而是城市热环境中最被低估的基础设施之一。**

  我们往往从两个极端理解街树：要么把它当作美化立面的「绿化装饰」，要么把它当作碳汇与生态指标的抽象数字。但站在行人的尺度上，街树真正改变的是**阴影、风速、表面辐射与心理预期**——这些共同塑造了我们对一条街道是否「可步行、可停留、可忍受」的判断。

  在城市科学里，这类问题正在与热舒适、街景感知和数字孪生交汇。若只有卫星层面的温度图，却没有街道层面的感知与实测，我们很容易做出「技术上正确、体验上失败」的决策。

  On Substack I published a short essay on street trees and urban microclimate. The core claim is simple: **street trees are among the most underrated infrastructure in the urban thermal environment.**

  We often understand them at two unhelpful extremes: as decorative greenery, or as abstract carbon and ecology metrics. At pedestrian scale, trees change **shade, wind, surface radiation, and psychological expectation**—together shaping whether a street feels walkable, lingerable, and bearable.

  In urban science, these questions intersect thermal comfort, street-view perception, and digital twins. Satellite-level heat maps without street-level sensing and experience invite decisions that are technically plausible yet experientially wrong.

  That is one reason I mirror Substack essays here: **same themes, different platforms, different audiences**—alongside longer research notes, without pushing every draft into the public blog list immediately.
