6 a.m. I checked my inbox and found another rejection letter.

Rejections are pretty normal in academic publishing, and this has happened to me many times before. Still, it makes me anxious and nervous, because I put so much effort into the work and had been waiting for that message for so long. For the whole day, I felt tired and just wanted to sleep.

The truth is that no research is perfect. Even the best papers can be criticized for many shortcomings. It is very frustrating when reviewers don't share your opinions, especially for young scholars, who really need recognition along the way and can easily feel that nothing they have done has succeeded.

That is why I think a resilience system for one's life is so important, and it is what I want to focus on in this blog. Everyone knows we have to suffer before we succeed, and that only after climbing over the mountain do we see the sunlight. But how to actually do it, and how to think about it, is still the hard part.

First, we need to get over it. Bad news in our careers brings real physical and mental pain, so we need to deal with our own feelings first.

Second, we need to take real action to solve the problem, rather than staying frustrated or just waiting and complaining. Only by trying again and working hard can we see what we can do and actually move toward a better situation.

Third, we need a long-term plan and strategy, so that our resilience system is sustainable and doesn't get crushed by the next failure.

This rejection won't be the last one. But with a system like this, I hope each one hurts a little less and teaches me a little more.
