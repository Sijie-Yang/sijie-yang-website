Streetscapes shape how hot or comfortable a city *feels*, yet measuring that potential across whole neighbourhoods has long been slow, costly, and tied to field campaigns. **Thermal Affordance** asks whether the visual information already embedded in street view imagery can serve as a scalable proxy for thermal comfort potential—linking what people see to what they are likely to experience outdoors.

We introduce *thermal affordance* as the integrated capacity of a streetscape—through its visible geometry, greenery, shading, and material cues—to support or hinder human thermal comfort. The **Visual Assessment of Thermal Affordance (VATA)** framework combines street view imagery, online and in-field surveys, and statistical learning: image features (IF) and visual-perceptual indicators (VPI) are chained to predict affordance scores, then validated against on-the-ground comfort measurements.

The approach is designed to be **cost-effective, city-scale, and interpretable**. Beyond prediction, VATA supports geospatial mapping of thermal comfort potential, model updating for long-term monitoring, and a two-stage inference workflow (IF–VPI–VATA) to guide streetscape improvements—informing design toward more sustainable, liveable, and resilient urban environments.

This work sits within the broader **City in Sight** programme as the street-level channel for linking visible urban form to human-centred environmental experience.
